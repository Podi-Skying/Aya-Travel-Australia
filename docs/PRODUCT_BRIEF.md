# Product Brief

## Purpose
Create a travel-planning website that combines itinerary, transport, budget, reliable travel information, sharing, and AI assistance. The first test should present the owner's real trip; broader Australian trip planning comes later.

## Users and access
- Initial users: owner, Aya, and invited friends.
- Later audience: international travellers, international students in Australia, and families.
- Owner login is required. Visitors need no account.
- The owner edits the main itinerary; visitors view a shared link and can submit activity/date interest.
- Primary interface language: Traditional Chinese. Keep official place names and source links in English where appropriate.

## Initial trip and constraints
- Sydney: 3–6 November 2026 (first segment).
- Tasmania: 6–27 November 2026.
- The earlier 25 October–2 November plans were removed (the trip had not started); those attractions are kept as an unscheduled pool (decision of 2026-10-22).
- UTAS Hobart Apartments costs AUD $0 for the owner and Aya only while they stay there. Other nights require separate accommodation budgeting.
- Zayden may drive Monday–Wednesday and JJ Saturday–Sunday, but neither availability nor a full-day commitment is confirmed. Do not rely on either driver until the exact date and time window are confirmed.
- Use the transport mode that best fits each region and date: driving, ferry, public transport, or walking.
- Split fuel, parking, ferry, and similar shared vehicle costs among participants in the relevant driving leg.

## Product goals
1. Make each day's activities, travel, timing, costs, and warnings easy to understand on mobile and desktop.
2. Let the owner generate and edit an itinerary with AI, compare alternatives, and restore earlier versions.
3. Show data sources, freshness, uncertainty, and feasibility.
4. Share a view-only itinerary and collect aggregate activity interest.
5. Keep essential itinerary information available offline, with a visible last-sync time.
6. Prioritise immersive maps and animation without sacrificing readability, accessibility, safety information, or mobile performance.

## Constraints and boundaries
- Project budget: AUD $0–500.
- Private testing first; no urgent public launch deadline.
- AI may suggest activities and link to external booking pages, but must not book or pay for the user.
- The first version centres on the real trip. General Australia-wide, arbitrary-date planning is a later expansion.
- Real-time data depends on provider access, licensing, reliability, and cost; unavailable data must be labelled rather than implied to be live.
